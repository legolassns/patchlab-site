Knowledge base

Custom patch guides

Everything we have learned making patches, organised so it can be consulted. Not a blog: a technical knowledge base on how custom patches are chosen, designed, produced and applied.

Why this section exists

Anyone ordering patches for the first time faces technical decisions they have no way to assess: embroidery or woven, merrow border or laser cut, sew-on or hook-and-loop backing, what minimum size keeps text legible. The consequences of a wrong choice only become visible on delivery, when changing it costs a new order.

These guides exist to make those decisions verifiable before production. They are written by the workshop that makes the patches, not by a marketing department: they also describe the cases where a technique is not the right choice, because that is the information a decision actually requires.

Frequently asked

Questions about these guides

  • Who writes these guides?

    The Euroricami workshop that produces the patches. Every technical statement describes work actually carried out: where a figure depends on the specific project (lead times, minimums, feasibility) we say so instead of giving a number that would be valid for nobody. See how the workshop is organised.

  • Are they meant to sell or to inform?

    To inform. Several guides explicitly explain when a technique should not be chosen and point to an alternative, even where that is less profitable for us: a patch produced with the wrong technique becomes a complaint, not a customer who reorders.

  • Can I use this information to compare suppliers?

    Yes, and we think that is the right way to use it. A quote only becomes comparable once you know which variables drive it: technique, size, number of colours, border type, backing type, quantity. That is exactly what the glossary is for.

  • How often are they updated?

    Guides are updated when something changes in production, or when a recurring customer question shows that an explanation was not clear. We do not publish on a fixed schedule to fill an editorial calendar.

  • I can't find the answer I need. What now?

    Write to us describing the project: the request form works for a technical question too, not only for a quote. The questions we receive most often become new entries in these guides.

Read enough to decide?

Tell us about the project: logo, approximate quantity, dimensions and intended date of use. If the technique you have in mind is not the right one, we will tell you before producing anything.
