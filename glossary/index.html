Custom patch glossary

Thirty-four technical terms that can appear in a quote, a production sheet or a conversation with a supplier. Knowing them lets you compare different offers on real grounds, not only on the final price.

How it is organised

Six groups, in logical order: first what is produced, then with what, how it is finished, how it is applied, how the artwork is prepared and how the result is checked.

  • Techniques — the production methods
  • Materials and components — bases, threads, embroidery structure
  • Borders and finishes — how the perimeter is closed
  • Backings and application — how the patch reaches the garment
  • Artwork and files — from logo to production file
  • Production and quality — sample, run, reorder, control
Group 1

Techniques

Patch

A decorative or identifying element produced separately and then applied to a garment by sewing, heat or hook-and-loop. Also called a badge; in technical and commercial use, "patch" prevails.

Embroidered patch

A patch made by stitching thread point by point onto a textile base, following a machine file. The thread adds thickness: the result has relief and a physical substance that fabric prints do not have. Dedicated page.

Woven HD

A patch whose design is formed within the interlacing of threads rather than on top of the fabric, using fine threads that hold more detail than embroidery. Flat surface, suited to thin lines and small text. Dedicated page.

Sublimation

A printing process in which ink turns to gas and penetrates the textile fibre, with no limit on the number of reproducible colours. Allows gradients and photographic images. Dedicated page.

PVC patch

A patch moulded in soft rubber with solid colours and a sculpted raised surface, waterproof and abrasion resistant. Used where fabric would wear out too quickly. Dedicated page.

Direct embroidery

Embroidery worked straight onto the finished garment, without producing a separate patch to apply. An alternative to a patch when the garment allows it and the mark does not need to be removable.

Group 2

Materials and components

Twill

A diagonally woven fabric used as the base of embroidered patches, valued for dimensional stability and precise stitch definition. It is the reference base when clean outlines are needed.

Felt

A non-woven base, softer and thicker than twill, used when a more crafted or vintage look is wanted. Less precise on fine detail, warmer to the eye and to the touch.

Polyester thread

The standard embroidery thread in patch production, chosen for wash resistance and colour stability in light. Alternative threads are considered for particular aesthetic requirements.

Stitch density

The number of embroidery stitches per unit of area: it determines coverage, stiffness and thread consumption. Too low and the base shows through; too high and the piece becomes rigid.

Underlay

A layer of foundation stitches laid before the visible embroidery, stabilising the fabric and supporting the final coverage. Invisible in the finished piece, but its absence shows.

Silhouette

The outer profile of the patch: either a regular geometric shape or a contour that follows the logo artwork. The silhouette determines which border finishes are technically possible.

Group 3

Borders and finishes

Merrow border

A cord stitched along the patch perimeter, suited to simple shapes such as circles, ovals and regular shields. On irregular silhouettes it cannot turn cleanly.

Laser cut

An outline finished by laser following the contour of the artwork, suited to irregular or intricate silhouettes. It produces a crisp edge where a merrow border is not applicable.

Embroidered border

A perimeter closed by a band of stitches instead of a cord, allowing freer contours than a merrow border. Often combined with a laser-cut outer profile.

Group 4

Backings and application

Sew-on backing

A textile backing with no adhesive, meant to be sewn by hand or machine: the most durable option over time. It is the reference for daily-use uniforms and frequently washed garments.

Iron-on backing

A heat-fusible film applied to the back of the patch, which bonds to the garment under heat and pressure. Adhesion depends on the fabric and the temperature the garment tolerates. Dedicated page.

Hook and loop

A two-component fastening system: the hook side on the back of the patch and the loop side on the garment, allowing the patch to be swapped without sewing. Dedicated page.

Hook

The hooked side of the fastening, applied to the back of the patch: on its own it attaches to nothing and requires the matching loop. This is the most frequent misunderstanding in hook-and-loop orders.

Loop

The looped side of the fastening, applied to the garment: it is the part that receives a hook-backed patch. If the garment is not prepared, it has to be sewn on separately.

Heat press

A machine applying controlled heat and pressure, used to fix iron-on patches more evenly than a household iron. On larger supplies it makes a measurable difference to adhesion.

Group 5

Artwork and files

Digitising

Conversion of the logo into an embroidery machine file: it defines density, stitch direction, sequence and colour changes. It determines embroidery quality more than any other step.

Machine file

The file the embroidery machine executes, produced by digitising: it is the asset that makes a reorder identical to the original. If it is archived, the second order does not start from scratch.

Vector file

Artwork described by curves rather than pixels, scalable without loss of quality: the ideal starting format for a patch. Typical extensions: AI, EPS, PDF, SVG.

Raster file

An image made of pixels, whose usability depends on its resolution at the real print or embroidery size. As a practical reference: at least 300 dpi at final size.

Outlined text

Text converted into vector curves, so the typeface stays identical even where the font is not installed. It prevents automatic font substitution, which changes shape and spacing.

Pantone

A colour reference system used to declare a colour unambiguously across artwork, threads and printing. Especially useful where colour is binding for identity or regulation.

Colour rendering

The inherent difference between a colour seen on screen and the colour achieved on thread or fabric, assessable only on a real sample. No digital preview removes it entirely.

Group 6

Production and quality

Confirmation sample

A real piece produced and approved before the production run, fixing colours, dimensions, detail and backing type. It is the point where expectations become verifiable. Where it sits in the process.

Production run

The number of pieces produced in a single order: it affects unit cost and the viability of certain finishes. Some border finishes only become worthwhile above a certain threshold.

Reorder

A new production of a patch already made, starting from archived specifications instead of from the beginning of the process. Lead time and cost are lower than a first order only if the archive exists.

Dimensional tolerance

The small permissible deviation between the requested and the achieved size, inherent to any textile process. It should be declared in advance, not discovered on inspection.

Quality control

Verification of produced pieces against the approved sample, covering colour, dimensions, border finish and backing integrity. How it works in the workshop.

Selected external manufacturing

Work assigned to a qualified partner when the technique or volume requires it, while keeping control of specifications and quality. This is not brokering: the specifications remain ours.
